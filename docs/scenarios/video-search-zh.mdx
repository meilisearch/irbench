Scenario id: `media/video-search-zh`. It tests title search over a media catalogue: show names, episode numbers, people's names, and mixed Latin and Chinese text (`ukBboy` against `uk bboy`).

## Source

- **Dataset:** [C-MTEB/VideoRetrieval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/C-MTEB/VideoRetrieval) (titles, 1,000
  dev queries and their qrels), from Alibaba's
  [Multi-CPR](https://github.com/Alibaba-NLP/Multi-CPR), plus Multi-CPR train
  queries whose judged title is in the same corpus. All files are pinned by
  revision or commit and sha256 in `datasets/cmteb-video/dataset.yaml`.
- **License:** not specified (see below).
- **Dataset id:** `cmteb-video`.

## Size

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Titles | 100,579 (100,930 in the source; 351 exact duplicates merged into the smallest id, 6 judgments moved) |
| Dev queries (C-MTEB) | 1,000 |
| Train queries (Multi-CPR, judged title in the corpus) | 3,000, sampled from 8,449 |
| Judgments | one per query, grade 1 |

Median title: 24 characters, 81% with a space; 1,402 titles are over 200
characters (cast lists). Median query: 7 characters, 2.3% with a space.
Examples: `鸡西新闻2019`, `胜者为王2天下无敌正片`.

Queries carry a group, `dev` or `train`. The `dev` group is the C-MTEB task
itself, comparable with published C-MTEB numbers; the train queries are there to
cut noise (engines are not trained, so a train query is as good a test). The
sample is seeded (`20261031`). Text is kept as written: no full-width folding, no
case folding, no traditional/simplified conversion.

On video, the two groups are not equally hard. Keyword MRR@10 on the default
track: Elasticsearch 0.536 on `dev` against 0.176 on `train`, Qdrant 0.541
against 0.202 (on e-commerce the groups are close: 0.385 against 0.369 for
Elasticsearch). Many train queries are vague (`电影小孩子看的`, "a film for kids")
and are judged against one catalogue entry, often a long cast list: the judged
title shares no character with its query for 5.1% of train queries against 0.3%
of dev ones, and its median length is 38 characters against 24. The labels look
click-based. The scenario keeps all 4,000 queries: the train group is a harder,
noisier set of real queries, not a broken one. Read the `dev` row for numbers
comparable with C-MTEB, and expect the headline to sit well below it.

## Fields and modes

One searchable field, `title`. Modes: `keyword`, `semantic`, `hybrid`.
`filtered`, `faceted` and `sorted` are N/A: the data has an id and a title,
nothing else. Page sizes for the speed runs: 20 and 100.

## One judgment per query

Each query has exactly one title marked relevant and nothing marked irrelevant.
So every metric is the rank of that one title: MRR@10 is 1 / rank (0 past 10),
success@1 is "ranked first", recall@k is "in the top k". Every other title counts
as wrong, even when it is the same product from another seller or another clip of
the same show, so scores are lower bounds. judged@10 is at most 0.1 by
construction and is not shown.

The scenario page headlines **MRR@10**, the metric of the Multi-CPR paper, with
its 95% interval. A second table gives success@1, recall@10, recall@100 and
nDCG@10, each with its interval. A score marked ≈ is not significantly below the
column's best (paired randomization test, p ≥ 0.05): with one judgment per query
many differences are within noise, and the page says so instead of ranking them.

Measured on the 1,000 dev queries with two simple BM25 variants (one token per
character, two-character pieces), one run's MRR@10 is known to about ±0.027 and a
paired difference to about ±0.02. The scenario has 4,000 queries, which halves
both. Absolute scores depend on the 100k-title corpus (the Multi-CPR paper's BM25
on the full corpus is far lower); only the comparison between engines on this
corpus means anything.

## Semantic search

The vectors come from `multilingual-e5-small` (`intfloat/multilingual-e5-small`,
384 dimensions, MIT), pinned on the scenario. Queries are embedded with the
`query: ` prefix and titles with `passage: `. It was not trained on Multi-CPR;
the BGE Chinese models were, which would make the semantic side reflect that
training. Exact (brute-force) retrieval with it reaches about the score of a plain
keyword baseline on the dev queries, so hybrid fuses two comparable halves.
**Semantic and hybrid scores here are not comparable with the English scenarios.**

## Chinese settings

| Engine | default track | tuned track |
|---|---|---|
| Meilisearch | `localizedAttributes` locale `cmn` on the title, `locales: ["cmn"]` on keyword and hybrid requests (charabia: jieba segmentation) | usual sweep, no plugin |
| Elasticsearch | `index.analyzer: cjk` (two-character pieces); Elasticsearch ships no Chinese word analyzer | `compose.zh.yaml`: `analysis-smartcn` and `analysis-icu` (official) and IK (very popular); variants `standard`, `cjk`, `smartcn`, `icu_analyzer`, `ik_smart`, `ik_max_word` |
| Typesense | `index.searchable_fields.locale: zh` | usual sweep, no plugin |
| Qdrant | `index.bm25_options.tokenizer: multilingual` | usual sweep, no plugin |
| ParadeDB | `index.tokenizer: pdb.jieba` | its other built-in tokenizers, `chinese_lindera` and `chinese_compatible` |
| Postgres | `index.text_search_config: simple`: Postgres has no Chinese configuration, so an unspaced title is one token and keyword search finds little | `compose.zh.yaml`: PGroonga (`TokenBigram`, `TokenNgram`) and zhparser (very popular; built from pinned upstream commits) |

Plugins follow the rule in [Methodology](/methodology/parity#language-plugins-on-the-tuned-track).
On these scenarios a sweep variant is kept only when it is significantly better
than the default value (Holm-corrected paired test), and analyzers and tokenizers
are chosen on the keyword base.

## Licence and publication

Multi-CPR has no licence file and its README states no terms; the C-MTEB copies
have none either. **Runs of this scenario are internal**: the scenario sets
`publishable: false` and `irbench publish` refuses them. Build a local report with
`irbench report --results .irbench/runs`. Raw files are downloaded at prepare time
and never committed.

## Perturbations

None. `typo` is not how Chinese is mistyped (an input method picks the wrong
character for the right sound), and a scenario with `language: zh` that lists it
is rejected. `prefix` would cut nearly every query, since 97% have no space, and
its variants would mix into the headline score; a Chinese as-you-type scenario
would carry them separately.
